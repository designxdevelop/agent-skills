# International scope

Use this reference when evidence suggests non-US operations or audiences.

For EU/EEA coverage, verify GDPR Article 3: establishment, offering goods or
services to people in the Union, or monitoring their behavior there. Assess
targeting and monitoring facts; mere website availability is insufficient.
UK coverage needs a separate UK GDPR/PECR assessment. Do not equate EU GDPR
with every country's privacy law.

For each relevant market, research privacy notices and rights, tracking/device
access consent, children's data, marketing consent, recurring billing and
cancellation, and user-content procedures. Identify processors, international
transfers, and retention. Check thresholds and sector exceptions before
asserting applicability. Localize controls where justified; a universal consent
banner does not resolve every obligation.

Self-hosting fonts can eliminate a third-party request, but it does not establish
overall GDPR compliance. Verify font license terms and actual network behavior.
Investigate lawful basis, notices, vendor role, and transfers for remaining
external resources. Do not turn a German court's fact-specific font decision
into a universal US prohibition or automatic damages calculation.

Starting sources:

- [GDPR, including Article 3 and Recital 23](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).
- [EDPB territorial-scope guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-32018-on-the-territorial-scope-of-the-gdpr-article-3-version-adopted_en).
- [ICO guidance on PECR](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/).
